Quick reference — common decision points
♥
Cardiac: when to delay
  • Decompensated heart failure (NYHA IV, acute pulmonary oedema)
  • Unstable / severe angina, recent ACS (within 60 days)
  • Symptomatic or uncontrolled significant arrhythmia
  • Severe symptomatic valvular disease (esp. AS with HF)
  • Recent PCI: BMS <30 days; DES <3 months (ideally 6–12 months)
⬆
Hypertension thresholds
  • Proceed if BP <180/110 — optimise but don't delay
  • 180–200 / 110–120: discuss, optimise, reassess
  • >200/120: delay elective surgery, urgent review
  • No evidence cancelling stage 1–2 HTN improves outcomes (ESAIC 2024)
  • ACEi/ARB: hold 24h before elevated-risk surgery
⬡
Diabetes key rules
  • Target peri-op BGL 6–10 mmol/L
  • HbA1c >9% (75 mmol/mol): consider deferring elective
  • SGLT2i: cease 3 days pre major surgery; ketones day of
  • GLP-1: continue (2025) + 24h clear fluid diet pre-op
  • Metformin: hold day of surgery only; sulfonylureas: hold day of
✚
Anticoagulation snapshot
  • Warfarin: cease 5 days pre-op; INR <1.5 target
  • Apixaban/rivaroxaban: hold 1 day (low-bleed) / 2 days (high)
  • Dabigatran: hold 2–4 days by CrCl
  • No bridging for DOACs (PAUSE)
  • Bridge warfarin only if high-risk (mech MVR, recent VTE)
Functional capacity (METs)
Poor <4 METs — high risk
  • Cannot climb one flight of stairs
  • Cannot walk at 6 km/h on flat
  • Limited to basic ADLs
Moderate 4–10 METs
  • Climb a flight / walk uphill
  • Golf, doubles tennis, dancing
  • Walk 6 km/h on flat
Good ≥10 METs — low risk
  • Swimming, singles tennis, football
  • Jogging / running
  • Heavy manual labour
ACC/AHA 2024: assess functional capacity with the DASI questionnaire (preferred over clinical estimate). Open the DASI calculator → Consider BNP/NT-proBNP if elevated risk + poor functional capacity.

When to request further testing
ACC/AHA 2024: test only when the result will change management — typically RCRI ≥3 + poor functional capacity + elevated-risk surgery.
  • Stress echo/nuclear: symptomatic, known CAD, multiple risk factors
  • Echo: suspected severe valve disease, new HF, unexplained dyspnoea
  • BNP/NT-proBNP: elevated-risk patients; if high → cardiology
  • ECG: known CVD, age >45 with risk factors, arrhythmia symptoms

Active conditions — DELAY surgery
  • Unstable coronary syndrome — unstable/severe angina, MI <60 days
  • Decompensated HF — NYHA IV, worsening/new HF
  • Significant arrhythmia — high-grade AV block, Mobitz II/III, symptomatic VT, uncontrolled SVT, new rapid AF, symptomatic bradycardia
  • Severe valvular disease — symptomatic severe AS; severe MS with NYHA III–IV

Heart failure
  • Optimise GDMT pre-op (ACEi/ARB/ARNI, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i — note SGLT2i hold rules)
  • Echo if new/deteriorating symptoms; EF guides anaesthetic strategy
  • Continue ACEi/ARB peri-op for HFrEF (ACC/AHA 2024)
  • Continue beta-blockers — never withdraw suddenly
  • No active fluid overload before elective surgery
  • High risk: peri-op BNP, plan HDU/ICU
Valvular disease
Aortic stenosis ▾
  • Severe symptomatic AS — delay; refer for TAVI/AVR
  • Severe asymptomatic + normal LV: may proceed if echo <12 months (IIa)
  • Goals: sinus rhythm, adequate preload, avoid tachycardia & vasodilation
  • Caution with neuraxial unless haemodynamics tightly managed
Mitral regurgitation ▾
  • Chronic severe MR tolerated if asymptomatic + preserved LV
  • Symptomatic/LV dysfunction: optimise; consider repair pre-op
  • Goals: slightly elevated HR, afterload reduction, avoid high SVR
Mechanical prosthetic valves ▾
  • Continue warfarin for low-bleed procedures
  • Bridge with LMWH/UFH for high-bleed — mechanical MVR highest risk
  • Never use DOACs with mechanical valves
  • Haematology/cardiology co-management
Cardiac implantable devices (CIED)
  • Notify anaesthetics & theatre — interrogation may be required
  • Pacemaker-dependent + monopolar diathermy → device clinic advice
  • ICD: defibrillation may need temporary disabling intra-op
  • Follow NALHN/SA cardiology CIED perioperative pathway

Diabetes — perioperative management
ADS-ANZCA Perioperative Diabetes and Hyperglycaemia Guidelines (Adults) — November 2022 v2
ADS-ANZCA 2022ADS SGLT2i Alert 2022ANZCA GLP-1 2025
Target peri-op BGL 6–10 mmol/L. Hypoglycaemia (<4) is the main risk to avoid; short-term moderate hyperglycaemia is acceptable in stable patients.
Assessment checklist
  • HbA1c within 3 months — target <9% (<75 mmol/mol) for elective
  • Renal function (affects SGLT2i, DOAC, metformin)
  • Full medication list incl. combinations & insulin type
  • CGM / insulin pump — document & plan
  • Severe hypos in last 6 months / hypo awareness
  • Complications: autonomic neuropathy (postural BP), gastroparesis (aspiration), CVD

Respiratory disease
ESAIC 2024–25 · ANZCA PG07 2024 · ANZCA smoking guideline 2023
COPD
  • FEV1 <50% predicted = moderate–high risk
  • Optimise bronchodilators; regular SABA + LABA/LAMA
  • Active wheeze/infective exacerbation — delay elective
  • Smoking cessation ideally ≥8 weeks; any cessation helps
  • Consider pulmonary rehab if FEV1 <50% before major surgery
  • Home O₂? document flow; ABG if available
Asthma
  • Well-controlled asthma is not a contraindication
  • Optimise inhaler technique; step up if poorly controlled
  • Active wheeze / oral steroids — delay elective; respiratory review
  • Continue preventers including morning of surgery
  • Steroid cover if ≥5 mg prednisolone >3 months

Smoking cessation
ANZCA 2023: advocate cessation at every visit. SA Quitline 13 7848.
  • ≥8 weeks: marked reduction in respiratory complications
  • 4–8 weeks: improved wound healing; some respiratory benefit
  • <4 weeks: transient mucus rise but cardiovascular benefit remains
  • NRT & varenicline safe peri-op
OSA
  • STOP-BANG ≥3 → document & notify anaesthetics (open calculator)
  • Known OSA on CPAP: continue; bring own device
  • Suspected OSA: sleep study referral; flag risk

Drug adjustments in CKD
  • Dabigatran: contraindicated CrCl <30
  • Apixaban/rivaroxaban: reduce/caution <30
  • Enoxaparin: 1 mg/kg/day if CrCl <30; consider UFH
  • Metformin: cease <30; hold peri-op at 30–45
  • SGLT2i: unsuitable <30–45 (drug-specific)
  • NSAIDs: avoid in any peri-op CKD
  • Gabapentin/pregabalin: dose reduce substantially
Hepatic disease
Child-Pugh C (10–15) = very high mortality. Avoid elective surgery; hepatology input.
ClassScore30-d mortality
A5–65–10%

  • Coagulopathy: don't reflexively correct INR — discuss haematology
  • Platelets: neuraxial ≥80 (low evidence); major surgery ≥50
  • Ascites: SBP risk; avoid unnecessary bowel prep
  • Encephalopathy: avoid benzodiazepines, morphine
Pre-op anaemia optimisation
  • Target Hb ≥80 g/L most elective; ≥100 for cardiac/major vascular
  • Iron deficiency: IV iron if surgery <4–6 weeks (oral takes ~3 months)
  • ESAIC 2024: IV iron reduces transfusion — offer proactively
  • B12/folate: replace & recheck 4–6 weeks
  • EPO: consider in CKD anaemia if Hb <100 & surgery within weeks
  • Arrange via NALHN haematology / outpatient IV iron service

Airway assessment
  • Mallampati III/IV — increased difficult airway risk
  • Neck circumference >40 cm — strong predictor
  • Mouth opening, thyromental distance, neck extension
  • Snoring/OSA history, previous difficult airway
  • Document "potential difficult airway — senior anaesthetist" if applicable
OSA management
  • STOP-BANG ≥3 → flag (open calculator)
  • Known OSA on CPAP: continue; bring device on admission
  • Untreated known OSA: document severity (AHI); specialist review for major
  • Suspected: sleep study if time; flag anaesthetics if urgent
  • OHS: SpO₂ <94% room air → ABG, pulmonary review
Aspiration risk
Obesity + GLP-1 agonist = compounded aspiration risk — see GLP-1/SGLT2i page. Gastroparesis, reflux & poor glycaemic control add further risk.
  • GORD: PPI/ranitidine course pre-op; sodium citrate day of
  • Active GORD/hiatus hernia: document for anaesthetics
  • Consider RSI for severe obesity with reflux
Frailty & the older surgical patient
ACC/AHA 2024 (frailty as risk modifier) · ANZCA PG07 2024 · Clinical Frailty Scale (Rockwood)
ACC/AHA 2024CFS (Rockwood)
ACC/AHA 2024: frailty is now an explicit risk modifier in perioperative cardiovascular assessment. Frailty independently predicts mortality, delirium, institutional discharge & complications — assess routinely in older patients regardless of RCRI.
Clinical Frailty Scale (CFS)
CFSDescriptor
1Very fit
2Well
3Managing well

CFS ≥5 = frail → higher peri-op risk; consider shared decision-making, prehabilitation, geriatric/perioperative medicine input.
Assessment domains
  • Cognition: screen for impairment & delirium risk (4AT, Mini-Cog)
  • Function: ADLs/IADLs, mobility, falls history
  • Nutrition: unintentional weight loss, low albumin
  • Polypharmacy: deprescribing opportunities; anticholinergic burden
  • Sarcopenia: grip strength, gait speed
  • Social: supports, discharge planning early
Optimisation & planning
  • Prehabilitation: exercise, nutrition, anaemia correction
  • Delirium prevention bundle; avoid high-risk drugs (benzos, anticholinergics)
  • Medication review & deprescribing
  • Shared decision-making re: goals & expected functional outcome
  • Early involvement of perioperative medicine / geriatrics for CFS ≥5
  • Consider HDU and explicit post-op delirium/mobility plan

Anticoagulation — perioperative management
CHEST 2022 · PAUSE study · ACC/AHA 2024 · BRIDGE trial
CHEST 2022PAUSEACC/AHA 2024
High thrombotic risk → haematology/cardiology co-management: mechanical heart valves, recent VTE (<3 months), antiphospholipid syndrome, CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≥7. Stratify with the CHA₂DS₂-VASc and HAS-BLED calculators.
Warfarin
  • 1
    Cease 5 days pre-op (adjust for weekends)
  • 2
    INR day before — if >1.5 consider vitamin K 1–2.5 mg oral
  • 3
    Bridging decision — most AF patients do NOT need bridging (BRIDGE)
  • 4
    Resume post-op at usual dose once haemostasis achieved; therapeutic INR in 5–7d

High aspiration risk — modified approach
May need RSI or awake intubation; flag anaesthetics:
  • Morbid obesity (BMI ≥40)
  • Known/suspected gastroparesis
  • Symptomatic GORD / active reflux
  • GLP-1 agonist use
  • Diabetic autonomic neuropathy
  • Large/symptomatic hiatus hernia
  • Previous bariatric surgery
  • Opioid dependence
  • Raised ICP
  • Pregnancy >20 weeks
Medications morning of surgery
  • Essential meds with small sip (<30 mL) water — allowed within 2h window
  • Document which meds taken AM of surgery in referral
  • BP meds: follow hold/continue rules
  • Inhalers: always take
  • Insulin: 80% basal, hold short-acting

Recommended tests
Tests for all surgery grades
Offer / consider regardless of grade
  • HbA1c — offer to people with diabetes not tested in last 3 months. Do NOT routinely offer to people without diagnosed diabetes.
  • Pregnancy test — sensitively ask all women of childbearing potential on day of surgery; test with consent if any doubt. Use a documented local protocol.
  • Echocardiography (resting) — not routine. Consider if heart murmur + cardiac symptom, or signs/symptoms of heart failure — do an ECG first and discuss with anaesthetist.
Do NOT routinely offer
  • Urine dipstick — only midstream M/C/S if a UTI would change the decision to operate
  • Sickle cell test — instead ask about personal/family history; liaise with sickle cell service if known disease
  • Chest X-ray — not routine before surgery
  • HbA1c in non-diabetics
Anticoagulants: routine haemostasis tests do not assess DOAC effect — DOAC levels can't be measured by routine coagulation testing. Make an individualised plan per local guidance (see Anticoagulation page).

  • Cardiac symptoms

  • RCRI ≥2

Chest X-ray
  • New/deteriorating cardiopulmonary symptoms
  • Known/suspected intrathoracic pathology
  • Major cardiothoracic surgery
  • Not routine (ESAIC 2024)
Echocardiogram
  • Suspected valvular disease
  • Unexplained dyspnoea/orthopnoea/oedema
  • New or deteriorating HF
  • Elevated BNP in elevated-risk patient
  • RCRI ≥3 + poor function + high-risk surgery
Biomarkers (BNP, troponin)
ACC/AHA 2024 (new): consider BNP/NT-proBNP in elevated-risk patients for intermediate–high risk surgery; if high → cardiology ± post-op troponin surveillance.
  • BNP >92 pg/mL; NT-proBNP >300 pg/mL = elevated
  • Post-op troponin surveillance: RCRI ≥3 or high-risk surgery
Group & hold / crossmatch
  • Expected significant loss (cardiac, vascular, liver, obstetric)
  • G&H for lower expected loss
  • Crossmatch if transfusion anticipated
  • Irregular antibodies → early SA Pathology LMH notification

Perioperative implications
  • Coagulopathy: elevated INR ≠ safe to correct routinely — discuss haematology before FFP/Vit K
  • Platelets: neuraxial ≥80×10⁹/L; major surgery ≥50
  • Ascites: SBP risk; careful fluid balance; avoid hyponatraemia
  • Encephalopathy: avoid benzodiazepines, high-dose opioids, morphine
  • Renal: HRS risk — avoid nephrotoxins, contrast; careful diuresis
  • Drugs: hepatically-metabolised agents — dose reduce (midazolam, fentanyl, local anaesthetics)
  • Nutrition: high protein-calorie needs; dietitian input pre-op
